# Square Surround and Spatial Motion v1

An Audio or MIDI Instrument track can opt into Square Surround when its named Mono or Stereo bus feeds a named 5.1 Side or Rear destination. Its input remains mono or separated stereo through the track inserts. The matrix runs after track inserts and volume and before routed Group and Master processing. Clip angular spatialization, implicit stereo folding, legacy analysis-mean routing and unknown/discrete bed inputs are incompatible. Direct and matrix bed routes retain their established processing. Legacy Track Azimuth and Width settings and their curves remain stored for explicit return to the angular policy.

X is rightward and Y is forward, both in −1…+1. Virtual anchors are L(−1,+1), C(0,+1), R(+1,+1), then Side(±1,0) or Rear(±1,−1). Angles here describe square coordinates, with physical layout roles taken from the bus. Let r=max(|X|,|Y|). Interpolate directional **power** linearly between adjacent anchors bracketing atan2(X,Y), with wrap through back center. Each of the five channels receives diffuse power (1−r)/5 plus r times its directional share. Square-root power yields gains with a unit squared sum. The center has five equal gains, front-center is C alone, and back-center is an equal pair of the chosen surrounds. LFE is silent in this matrix. Existing independent bed/source LFE remains available on compatible routing. Track LFE send and additional object buses are outside this v1 policy.

Stereo treats L and R as distinct objects. With h=Spread/2, their X coordinates are X(1−h)−h and X(1−h)+h at the same Y. Each column has power 1/2. Coherent inputs can sum, so actual output level still depends on source correlation and mix headroom. The producer evaluates the matrix on the established project-anchored 32-frame grid with exact key boundaries after track latency. Each coefficient moves toward its target over 10 ms. Each resulting input column is normalized per sample to its declared power, keeping the transition continuous. Stereo monitoring uses the existing separate monitor map and has no influence on WAV output.

Manifest 19 uses `square-surround-v1` only if a track in the original, a variation or a frozen recipe uses the policy. An opted-in frozen take uses recipe 10 and `square-surround-takes-v1`. It freezes the full mix and inherits compensated source dependencies. Historical encodings, processing policies and recipe hashes retain their existing branches. Live controls carry the policy and position with the track ID. Changing mode is structural. Position/spread and motion curves update the prepared graph off the native callback. The device callback consumes published PCM.

## Editing path and Touch

Choose a Mono or Stereo Audio or MIDI Instrument Track from Edit and route it to a 5.1 Side or Rear Master or Group. Open **Track options → Square Surround**. The floating, resizable window previews new speaker shares alongside the current routing before **Enable XY**. Press the same control to return to the prior policy. Existing angular parameters remain in the model.

Drag the object or use arrows and Shift arrows for fine moves. Double-click the field to add a joined X/Y point at the project playhead. Selected points accept drags, Delete, and the Motion menu's Linear, Hold and Smooth interpolation. Shift-drag selects a rectangle, and Command-A/C/V selects or copies/pastes the path into another compatible track at its playhead. Enter opens exact seconds and X/Y for one selected point. Numeric entry and Undo/Redo use the common document. Grid snap uses the existing Edit settings. Point motion remains project-relative as audio and MIDI clips move.

Each track keeps its existing collapsed Volume-first lane. Square Surround registers Position X, Position Y and Stereo Spread on compatible Stereo tracks. Read evaluates all enabled curves. The panner combines the independent X/Y streams for its path and evaluates the playing position from the shared playhead. Speaker bars depict each role's target share of input power. The linked stereo markers show the two distinct source locations. The underlying curve interpolates by project-frame time. Smooth uses cubic smoothstep, including its exact phase when divided by a crop or a Touch boundary. Hold applies until the next key.

The panner's Touch button is a paired gesture arm. During playback the first drag binds the owner track, stable X/Y targets, playback generation and graph epoch. The producer coalesces queued moves until the next source block, then applies the pair at the first unrendered Track output-aligned frame. It holds between applied values. Release returns to the underlying Read value at the exact endpoint 100 ms later, through the same scheduling and coefficient smoothing. Disarm or close the window to release. Stop, seek or graph replacement closes the applied prefix. A changed document/variation cannot adopt a stale gesture. Attachment, audio/MIDI capture and frozen replay use the existing distinct transport contexts.

One bounded receipt carries at most 2,044 applied points, with a versioned 12-byte record for frame and float X/Y values. It stays below 34 KB including its envelope. A matching acknowledgment drops the payload from repeated status. The session commits both curves in one Undo and retains them through the normal autosave pipeline. Pending save/close waits for that result. The point model retains the existing 2,048 points per curve and 8,192 per mix bounds. A rejected retention reports why. The helper cannot certify a gesture that produced no audio frames. Losing the audio helper before its receipt can lose the in-flight pass.

Physical mapping is covered by role order and WAV masks in the software tests. Human listening, calibrated speaker checks and receiver persistence have their own acceptance requirements.
